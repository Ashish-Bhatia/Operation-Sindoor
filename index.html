FILE 01

The attack that preceded the war

Twenty-six people were killed at Pahalgam on 22 April 2025. Accountability, attribution and advance warning are three different questions.

The UN Security Council publicly condemned the attack on 25 April and called on states to support efforts to bring perpetrators, organizers, financiers and sponsors to justice. Its statement did not attribute the attack to a named organization. Source S01 ↗

India's Foreign Secretary attributed the operation to Pakistan-linked Lashkar-e-Taiba attackers in the 7 May briefing, citing an alleged TRF responsibility claim, communications evidence and developing eyewitness identifications. These are official attribution statements. The publicly released statement did not contain the complete case exhibits. Source S04 ↗

The case moved beyond May 2025

On 15 December 2025, the National Investigation Agency announced a chargesheet against LeT/TRF and six other accused. On 6 July 2026 it announced a supplementary chargesheet naming Hafiz Saeed. These materially advance the documented prosecution record. They do not, alone, establish that a court has found the accused guilty. S15 S16

Evidence boundary: The complete filed chargesheets, forensic exhibits, witness records and any latest signed orders have not been independently inspected in this edition. The case against individuals must not be presented as adjudicated.

Operation Mahadev

The Indian government reported killing three alleged Pahalgam attackers during Operation Mahadev on 28 July 2025 and described forensic ballistics and witness identification. That public account creates a concrete testable lead. The underlying chain-of-custody and expert forensic reports remain to be obtained. S21 ↗

The warning question

A failure to prevent an attack does not, by itself, prove that officials ignored a specific actionable warning. The public evidence reviewed here does not yet establish the contents, addressees or timing of any such warning. This remains a major unresolved security question.

FILE 03

The nine opening targets

Named in India's 7 May account. Listing a site and its alleged affiliation does not establish its actual function or the effects of a strike.

India initially said nine sites had been targeted and no Pakistani military facilities had been struck. Pakistan's official account disputed the described nature of the targets and alleged civilian harm. Reuters documented the locations as claims supplied by India. Each location needs independent historical facility evidence, before-and-after imagery, and incident-specific civilian impact accounting. S03 S06 S17

FILE 04

The aircraft question

There is a critical difference between acknowledging losses and verifying a specific number and model.

8 May 2025 / Reuters

Foreign officials reported losses

Two US officials told Reuters that Pakistan had brought down at least two Indian jets. One said a Rafale was among them. It is material independent reporting, but its underlying classified assessment and serial-level proof were not made public in the article.

S11
31 May 2025 / Reuters

India's CDS acknowledged air losses

General Anil Chauhan acknowledged initial Indian losses, declined to provide an aircraft tally, and said India changed tactics. This defeats categorical claims that India sustained no initial losses, but does not validate every Pakistani figure.

S12
Current finding: Initial Indian air losses are established by an Indian on-record acknowledgement. The exact tally, specific aircraft types and exact causes remain unresolved in this evidence pass. No Pakistani aircraft-loss tally is independently accepted without its own evidence.

Claims about fighter quality or Chinese versus French weapons cannot be settled solely by a shootdown allegation. Engagement circumstances, tactics, situational awareness, attribution and forensic evidence matter.

FILE 05

The wider confrontation

The opening strike phase and later attacks on military facilities must be assessed as different operations.

Indian position

Limited opening action, later retaliation

India characterized 7 May as a measured counterterrorism response and subsequently described strikes on Pakistani military facilities after escalation. India's retrospective summaries assert extensive effectiveness. These operational results require site-specific verification.

S03S09
Pakistani position

Sovereignty violation and counteroperation

Pakistan described the Indian strikes as unlawful aggression and announced Operation Bunyan-ul-Marsoos on 10 May as self-defence. Claimed damage to Indian military assets has to meet the same evidence threshold as Indian strike claims.

S08

What imagery does, and does not, show

Commercial satellite-image reporting described physical damage at Pakistani military installations, including Nur Khan and Murid. Damage to a building or runway is not by itself proof of destroyed aircraft, killed personnel, loss of command capability or the strategic reason for cessation. Original image products and licensing have not yet been obtained for independent pixel analysis. S18 S19

Do not merge outcomes: Each side's initial objectives, physical effects, aircraft losses, civilian harm and eventual strategic consequences are assessed separately. No complete victory rating is issued in this edition.
FILE 06

Who stopped the fighting?

The cessation was real. The claim that one actor alone caused it is not yet established.

US public account

President Trump said the US mediated the agreement. Secretary Rubio described senior-level communications with officials from both governments over 48 hours.

S10
Indian public account

India described a direct DGMO conversation and an understanding effective from 17:00 IST on 10 May. Senior Indian officials rejected the claim that external pressure forced the decision.

S09S22

Diplomatic engagement and direct military communication may both have occurred. Their relative importance is an evidentiary question. Pakistan's response independently confirms acceptance of a ceasefire understanding and presents its counterstrikes under Article 51. The public statements do not supply a complete call-by-call record of the negotiation. S08 ↗

FILE 07

Claims versus evidence

An evidence finding and a confidence level are separate. High-confidence proof that a claim was made is not high-confidence proof that it was true.

Counts describe the editorial status of claims in this first-edition register, not the number of military incidents. Ratings are evidence judgments, not mathematical probabilities.

FILE 11

How we distinguish fact from claim

Symmetrical standards apply to every government, journalist, claimant, armed force and outside observer.

Official statements prove what an authority stated

A dated briefing is strong evidence of its author's public position. It is not automatically evidence that every weapon, casualty figure or target description was accurate.

One source does not become many through repetition

Multiple outlets citing the same anonymous official are counted as one underlying account. Source independence is evaluated by provenance, not quantity.

A physical effect is narrower than a strategic effect

A before-and-after image showing roof damage cannot independently establish deaths, aircraft losses, military effectiveness or the motive behind subsequent diplomacy.

Charges are not convictions

Accusations against identifiable people remain allegations until proven through due process. Public records are presented at their correct procedural stage.

Safety and corrections

We omit current exploitable defence vulnerabilities, sensitive deployment details and unnecessary personal information. Every revised finding should be traceable to new evidence and a dated editorial correction.

Scope of this edition: This is the initial documentary foundation. Uninspected chargesheet exhibits, full source videos, raw satellite products, casualty reconciliation, legal analysis and economic estimates remain pending. This site does not update automatically.
