# Operation Sindoor: investigative checkpoint 06

**Cutoff:** 19 October 2026  
**Continuation:** [Checkpoint 05](checkpoint-05.md) and earlier research are retained; only specifically identified pending findings are revised.  
**Status:** Independent public-record inquiry. This is **not** a completed adjudication, a court judgment or a military battle-damage assessment.

## Executive findings

This pass substantially strengthens the UN legal and diplomatic source record. The principal additions are authenticated UN Security Council correspondence **S/2025/289, 290, 299, 305 and 308**, the pre-conflict **S/2025/252 and 263**, paired original human-rights **Special Procedures allegation letters AL IND 10/2025 and AL PAK 10/2025**, a separate domestic-response communication **AL IND 8/2025**, and India's **4 December 2025 Rajya Sabha answer no. 554**.

**Legal finding qualified:** Pakistan's Security Council correspondence clearly and repeatedly invokes **Article 51** and the 16 May letter expressly says its 10 May counteroperation occurred in self-defence. The earlier 6–7 May letters primarily *reserve* a future right to respond, while the 16 May letter describes action already undertaken. **Do not equate advance reservation of a right to the UN Charter's reporting requirement for measures already taken**; evaluate the reporting obligation, timing and substantive law separately. The OHCHR experts characterised Pakistan as having notified the Council, but did not thereby hold all Pakistani strikes lawful. Their India letter says they did not locate a corresponding Indian Article 51 notification, as of **16 October 2025**. That is the experts' dated observation, not a conclusive universal negative about every later document.

**Humanitarian finding qualified:** Independent UN experts formally asked both states to explain potential rights/IHL violations. Neither letter is a binding determination. The third communication reports approximately 2,800 arrests after Pahalgam; it is a documented *allegation* rather than independently verified arrest, detention or conviction accounting.

**Cessation:** Pakistan's formally circulated 13 and 16 May letters both attribute the cessation to facilitation by **several friendly countries**. India's 4 December parliamentary answer says the cessation **understanding was arrived at directly between the two Directors General of Military Operations**. These propositions are not logically inconsistent: facilitation and the direct military agreement may coexist. Neither alone establishes who exercised decisive political pressure or whether external participation was accepted as formal mediation.

The **S/2025/290 NSC annex** invokes Article 51 as a *reserved right* to respond at its chosen time, place and manner. Its pre-coun­teroperation timing must not be erased by the later S/2025/308 description of an operation already executed.

**S/2025/308 civilian breakdown:** Pakistan alleged **40 civilians killed**, including **7 women and 15 children**, and **121 civilians injured**, including **10 women and 27 children**. This is a **13–16 May cumulative government claim circulated 16 May**. The child and woman descriptors should not be assumed mutually exclusive or added to the parent totals. Nor should the report be added as a new set of 40 fatalities to the existing May 13 Pakistan casualty record; it is a restatement of the same figures until victim-level reconciliation proves otherwise. An actual cause-of-death roster remains unavailable.

The UN Security Council [held closed consultations on 5 May 2025](https://main.un.org/securitycouncil/en/events/indiapakistan), **requested by Pakistan**, but [the contemporary proceedings review](https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/monthly-forecast/2025-06/status-update-since-our-may-forecast-7.php?print=true) describes **no resulting Council statement**. A closed Council consultation is not an authorisation of either party's subsequent use of force.

## II. Article 51: five distinct propositions

1. **Invocation in a letter**: Verified for Pakistan on 6–7 May (S/2025/289, S/2025/290).
2. **Reporting measures already taken**: The 16 May S/2025/308 expressly identifies the 10 May Pakistani counteroperation and invokes Article 51. Whether the reporting satisfied every *promptness* requirement is a further legal question; a six-day interval must not be silently treated as immediate or necessarily a legal failure without examining the circumstances.
3. **India's public self-defence rationale**: Its 7 May official account framed strikes as a response to Pahalgam and an attempt to avert future attacks, as recorded in [the original Indian MEA briefings](https://www.mea.gov.in/media-briefings.htm?dtl/39464/Transcript_of_Special_briefing_on_OPERATION_SINDOOR_May_07_2025). Publicly invoking self-defence is different from submitting an identifiable Article 51 Security Council report.
4. **Negative documentary finding**: [Independent UN mandate-holders writing on 16 October 2025, AL IND 10/2025](https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TMResultsBase/DownLoadPublicCommunicationFile?gId=30361), explicitly said India had **not notified** the Council of an Article 51 claim. This is a **dated expert observation**. A comprehensive document-symbol/index audit for India and subsequent submissions remains outstanding. Do not present a negative search result as a timeless conclusive legal finding.
5. **Substantive legality**: Reporting alone proves neither necessity and proportionality in jus ad bellum nor distinction, precautions or proportionality in IHL. Independent UN experts' legal interpretations are authoritative advocacy by mandate-holders, **not an ICJ or Security Council adjudication**, including in disputed areas concerning self-defence against non-state actors.

## III. Paired OHCHR Special Procedures correspondence: reciprocal legal scrutiny

### [AL IND 10/2025, 16 October 2025](https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TMResultsBase/DownLoadPublicCommunicationFile?gId=30361), 17 pages

**Issuer:** Special Rapporteurs/independent expert (counter-terrorism and human rights, right to environment, extrajudicial executions, international order, safe water and sanitation). **Type:** joint allegation letter requesting responses, **not a court ruling**. They condemn Pahalgam, question the legal basis for Indian strikes and India's asserted abeyance of the Indus Waters Treaty, express concerns about potential impacts on life, food, water and livelihood, and ask India for explanations. On pp. 3 and 7 they assert that India did not submit an Article 51 notification and had not publicly demonstrated that Pakistan's government had sent the perpetrators.

**Limit:** The experts' particular interpretation of state attribution for the right of self-defence should not be asserted as universal uncontested law. The letter explicitly frames alleged underlying facts conditionally and invites India's reply; it establishes legal concerns, not state responsibility, the actual hydrological effect, or a binding judgment. Treaty arbitration rulings have their own contested-jurisdiction status and different legal remit.

### [AL PAK 10/2025, 16 October 2025](https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TMResultsBase/DownLoadPublicCommunicationFile?gId=30432), 10 pages

**Issuer:** Special Rapporteurs/independent expert (counter-terrorism, extrajudicial executions, international order). **Type:** joint allegation letter; direct counterpart of the India letter. Its page 3 recognises Pakistan notified the Security Council of an asserted Article 51 defence and then separately identifies allegations of strikes affecting civilian homes, schools and religious sites in India, including the Poonch area. It requests explanations of target distinction, precautions, investigations, reparations and the basis for measures involving the **Simla Agreement, visas, aviation, trade, postal and maritime links**.

**Limit:** No location-level death toll, weapon attribution or completed illegality finding is authenticated by the mere existence of this letter. No conclusion of Pakistan's innocence follows from its Article 51 notice. The letters do not establish each incident's target status or ex ante operational knowledge.

### [AL IND 8/2025, 5 September 2025](https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TMResultsBase/DownLoadPublicCommunicationFile?gId=30287), 20 pages

**Type:** Separate communication on domestic post-Pahalgam counterterrorism responses. It cites information received alleging approximately **2,800 arrests** in Kashmir after 22 April, extended detention and ill-treatment of some people, demolitions of **at least nine homes** between 24–27 April and related due-process concerns. It additionally sets out other allegations about social and religious discrimination and removals.

**Source-critical warning:** These are **allegations brought to mandate-holders**; no arrest-list, person-level case audit, full government answer or independent underlying case-file reconciliation was obtained here. Do not convert the approximately 2,800 into confirmed unlawful detentions, convicted criminals, or a national disappearance count. Verified government security operations and constitutional rights can be discussed without presuming every detention unlawful.

**Replies:** The OHCHR communication portal's result rows displayed no linked replies for these three records at the time of this audit. This is a *portal access observation*, not proof no government ever communicated privately or publicly elsewhere. Search other official replies before making a nonresponse conclusion.

## IV. Reciprocal official ceasefire documentation, not mutually exclusive

Pakistan [S/2025/305](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4082311/files/S_2025_305-EN.pdf) says friendly countries contacted it with de-escalation messages and the 10 May cessation resulted from that facilitation. [S/2025/308](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4082687/files/S_2025_308-EN.pdf) repeats the **several friendly countries** formulation.

India's original [Rajya Sabha written answer 554, 4 December 2025](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/269/AU554_Fdrh9b.pdf?source=pqars), by Minister of State Kirti Vardhan Singh, confirms India's position that Pakistan's DGMO approached India's DGMO on 10 May and that the **understanding was arrived at directly between the two DGMOs**. The answer also states that an **all-party briefing took place on 8 May** and later parliamentary/delegation briefings occurred; it does not provide verbatim international negotiator call logs.

**Result:** Multiple countries may have transmitted de-escalation messages, while military officials separately finalised an agreement directly. Neither state record proves who initiated the first contact across *all* diplomatic channels, nor the degree of decisive foreign coercion. Pakistan's S/305 and S/308 are two original documents but **one interested government's narrative**, not two independent corroborating states.

4. **Civilian victim ledger:** compare Pakistan's 40 killed/121 injured and subcategories to actual names, hospitals, medical files and site-specific incidents; keep separate from military and Pahalgam fatalities, and avoid double-counting.
5. **Domestic post-attack rights audit:** verify the asserted 2,800 detentions through official FIR/register, PSA/UAPA orders, hearing dates and court outcomes, with no identifying details about private uncharged persons.
6. **Diplomatic chronology:** obtain US, Pakistan and India contemporaneous readouts/telegrams for 8–12 May; distinguish coercion, facilitation and operative DGMO agreement.
7. **Battle damage:** carry forward the independent hull-number aircraft casualty lists, original imagery and airfield recovery timeline.
8. **Legal merits:** treat each attack under applicable jus ad bellum and IHL tests separately; do not substitute a UN mandate-holders' complaint or an Indus Waters arbitration award for an adjudication of Operation Sindoor's legality.

## Publication and methodology limits

The UN letters' original-host text was available through independently indexed PDF extracts, and several UN catalogue entries were inspected. Some full PDF URLs returned access errors through the web reader; full locally downloaded originals and hash verification **were not completed**. In contrast, the three OHCHR public PDF communications were opened through the original OHCHR communications archive, with identifiable page/line locations. The Indian parliamentary answer was indexed by its official Sansad PDF. All parties' accusations and underlying claims retain source-specific evidentiary grades.

No complete bilateral casualty count, aircraft tally, site-by-site illegality conclusion, judicial guilt finding in the Pahalgam prosecution, proof of a sole US broker or conclusive global Article 51-notification negative is warranted. No continuous autonomous research or monitoring has been configured.
