# Operation Sindoor: investigative checkpoint 11

**Evidence cutoff:** 19 October 2026  
**Continuity:** Extends [checkpoint 10](checkpoint-10.md) and the existing [aircraft-loss](aircraft-loss-review.md) and [airfield-damage](airfield-damage-review.md) dossiers.  
**Status:** Independent, incomplete public-record investigation. This is a *claim-provenance and evidence-separation* checkpoint, **not** a fresh aircraft wreckage authentication or original Ministry of Defence PDF inspection.

## Executive assessment

The newest consequential reporting is of India's **Ministry of Defence Annual Report 2025–26, released 13 September 2026**, specifying a purported **314 km S-400 surface-to-air kill** of a Pakistani **special-mission aircraft**. A government-hosted original PDF URL has been identified at [mod.gov.in/sites/default/files/Annual-report-2025-26.pdf](https://mod.gov.in/sites/default/files/Annual-report-2025-26.pdf), but direct attempts to open/download the PDF failed. Multiple contemporaneous journalists report the same range and description. **It is a later, more numerically precise version of an August–October 2025 Indian Air Chief claim of a large-aircraft interception at 'about / more than 300 km', not a second separately identified Pakistani aircraft**. Airframe type, serial, GPS crash site, independently authenticated wreckage and casualty list have not been supplied. Do **not** infer a specifically identified Saab-2000 AEW&C from the term 'special-mission'.

Meanwhile **Pakistan's publicly recorded aircraft score varies within May 2026**. A [7 May Pakistan state-broadcaster report of a military briefing](https://www.radio.gov.pk/07-05-2026/pakistan-completely-defeated-india-in-marka-e-haq-dg-ispr) says its Deputy Chief of Air Staff claimed eight **'fighter jets'**, while its enumerated list contains **seven manned fighters** (four Rafales, one Su-30, one Mirage 2000, one MiG-29) **plus one unmanned system**. A [11 May National Assembly Speaker official press release](https://na.gov.pk/en/pressrelease_detail.php?id=7811) instead says **seven 'enemy aircraft'**. Both are **officially attributable claims**, neither an independently authenticated aircraft serial roster. The internal manned-versus-unmanned denominator problem needs public clarification; **do not assume** the Speaker's seven is a retraction of the military's earlier eight without evidence.

A [3 October 2025 Indian Air Chief briefing reported by the Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/pakistani-fighter-jets-f-16s-destroyed-operation-sindoor-air-force-chief-10285337/) describes **two separate alleged loss channels**: (1) at least five fighters plus one large mission aircraft claimed destroyed **in the air**, and (2) a C-130, another AEW&C and four–five fighters **allegedly damaged or destroyed on the ground in multiple hangars/tarmac locations**. This language is not a verified sum and may mix evidence classes; avoid merging the airborne large plane with Bholari's suspected hangared aircraft. The physical collapse of a hangar does not prove an aircraft occupied it.

The [20 August 2026 NDTV first-person account of Air Marshal Nagesh Kapoor](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/operation-sindoor-pakistan-the-hangar-at-bholari-how-a-white-lie-sealed-a-mission-11933671) strengthens source attribution for an **Indian BrahMos strike mission against a Bholari hangar** but supplies no authenticated pre-impact hangar-interior inventory or post-impact serial numbers. The Indian commander and NDTV's satellite-image descriptions are not disinterested corroboration of the *specific Saab aircraft* being inside.

The study must not falsely claim to have read that PDF.

These are separate alleged modes of loss. Similar mission category **does not establish identical or different airframes**. Require tail number and event/track data.

## II. Pakistan's 2026 claim accounting is not stable

| Date | Government origin | Reported quantity | Internal categorization |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **7 May 2025** | [S/2025/290, Pakistan NSC statement](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4082002/files/S_2025_290-EN.pdf) | Five fighters claimed | Initial official claim; no independently verified five-crash roster |
| **7 May 2026** | [Radio Pakistan quoting Deputy Air Chief, lines 48–52](https://www.radio.gov.pk/07-05-2026/pakistan-completely-defeated-india-in-marka-e-haq-dg-ispr) | 'Eight fighter jets' | Actually seven **manned fighter models** (4 Rafales + Su-30 + Mirage 2000 + MiG-29) **and one multirole unmanned aircraft/system** |
| **11 May 2026** | [National Assembly Speaker's actual official release](https://na.gov.pk/en/pressrelease_detail.php?id=7811) | Seven 'enemy aircraft' | Does not give aircraft models or hull list, and does not explicitly reconcile the prior unmanned-system claim |

The **7 May 2026 Deputy Air Chief** also asserted that sixteen Indian airbases, BrahMos stockpiles, control centres and two S-400 batteries were hit. **Those are additional government strike claims**, not independent damage observations. No matching sixteen-base dated geolocated independent strike/damage register or verified destruction of two S-400 batteries has been obtained. The May 2025 Indian [PIB fact-check list](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/may/doc2025510552901.pdf) explicitly contested earlier social-media allegations that an Indian S-400 had been destroyed. A government fact-check establishes India's counterposition, not a forensic omniscient proof that every component was untouched.

**The Speaker's press release also alleges that Pahalgam was a false flag.** This is an **interested political assertion with no original substantiating evidence in that release**, and does not undo the separate NIA court prosecution or establish an alternative perpetrator theory. Do not promote it into the investigation's adopted finding.

## III. Bholari: what an Indian commander's account independently adds — and cannot show

[NDTV's 20 August 2026 interview-derived report](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/operation-sindoor-pakistan-the-hangar-at-bholari-how-a-white-lie-sealed-a-mission-11933671) names **Air Marshal Nagesh Kapoor**, then involved in the South Western Air Command, who says the mission was launched with Sukhoi-30MKI carrying BrahMos air-launched munitions. The commander narrates delaying a truthful timing update to the Air Chief so ready pilots could execute the strike. NDTV links the commander's account to a previously satellite-photographed **Bholari hangar roof breach and collapse**.

**Evidence classes:**

- **Government participant's recollection, quoted in a journalist interview:** strong evidence that Kapoor **said** these things about the mission and decisions; it is not a released targeting order or independent flight recorder.
- **Post-strike satellite-observed Bholari structural changes:** supports physical damage to a specific hangar, already documented by 2025 Washington Post imagery and later retrospective imagery.
- **Purpose and believed contents:** Kapoor/NDTV say India reasonably believed a Saab Erieye was there. **Belief before strike ≠ a tail-numbered aircraft actually inside at impact**.
- **No dated interior photograph, pre/post aircraft inventory, recovered airframe wreckage, independent Pakistan loss acknowledgment or 2026 maintenance/service serial ledger** was furnished.

**Finding:** Stronger provenance of India's hangar targeting purpose and execution, **no independent upgrade** of **C37** to 'a Saab destroyed in hangar'. Also do not conflate alleged Bholari **ground destruction** with the **314 km airborne interception**; these are separate asserted events until record-specific identification.

## IV. Independent open-source controls

The [Stimson Center's 'Four Days in May' (Christopher Clary, 2025)](https://www.stimson.org/2025/four-days-in-may-the-india-pakistan-crisis-of-2025/) analyses eyewitness/video wreckage sites and satellite images, drawing on Washington Post/New York Times reporting.

Key independent conclusions in that time-bounded survey:

- Indian loss evidence exists at **at least three distinct crash sites**, with some wreckage consistent with aircraft models in India's inventory; **Pakistan's full five, six, seven or eight** is not thereby verified.
- Visible Indian strikes caused localized physical damage at **multiple Pakistani air installations**, including runways and hangars.
- Images **did not** establish a jet necessarily occupied a damaged hangar. The analyst explicitly treated a Bholari Saab loss as **suggestive at best** in the 2025 public evidence.
- On May 11, visible runway patching at some Pakistan bases does **not** prove full return to service; later 2026 reconstruction imagery does **not** prove uninterrupted shutdown through the repair date.
- No evidence in that **2025** account adequately corroborated sweeping Pakistani claims of disabling large numbers of Indian bases. This finding is **time-limited**; it is not proof no Indian installation sustained any operational damage.

The independent [IISS 15 May 2025 regional assessment](https://www.iiss.org/online-analysis/online-analysis/2025/05/indiapakistan-drone-and-missile-conflict-differing-and-disputed-narratives/) similarly says Pakistan targeted air bases at Udhampur, Pathankot, Adampur and Bhuj, while India acknowledged **limited damage**. It reports Pakistan's then-dated cumulative **13 military** deaths, consistent with checkpoint 08's 14 May updated official count. These research assessments are **independent analytical syntheses**, but their use of original government briefings and press images must not be counted as two additional primary battlefield witnesses.

### Analytical boundary

Checkpoint 11 strengthens **precise attribution, chronological evolution and internal consistency testing** of aircraft-loss claims. It **does not establish** a newly verified Pakistani aircraft destruction, an additional Indian hull loss, the actual number of aircraft stored in Bholari, two destroyed Indian S-400 batteries, or a numerical 'winner'.

The best-supported summary remains: **some Indian combat aircraft losses and significant localized damage to Pakistani military facilities**, with serial-specific Pakistani fighter losses, exact comparative attrition, aircraft occupancy, downtime and broader strategic outcomes not independently settled.
