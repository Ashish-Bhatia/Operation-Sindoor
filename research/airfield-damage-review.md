# Pakistan airfield damage: historical imagery evidence assessment

**Checkpoint:** 02 · **Cutoff:** 19 October 2026  
**Nature:** Source-critical review of completed, historical 10 May 2025 strike effects. No current defensive locations, dispositions, vulnerabilities or targeting recommendations.

## Central finding

On [14 May 2025, the Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2025/05/14/india-pakistan-strikes-conflict-damage/) published a visual investigation based on **more than two dozen satellite frames and aftermath videos**. It described physical damage at **at least six Pakistani airfields**: three aircraft hangars, two runways, and two mobile buildings associated with the air force. The article reported that a Pakistani military spokesperson acknowledged hits at **five military bases and one civilian airport**, while disputing the effectiveness of many incoming weapons.

This is substantially stronger evidence than merely repeating an Indian post-strike claim.

### What the published investigation shows, and its limits

| Historic reported site | Publicly observable effect described by Post | Confidence in published historic damage | What is not established |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nur Khan (Rawalpindi area) | Damage to mobile structures; smoke in contemporaneous public video | High that structures were damaged | Whether specific command systems were operationally disabled, and for how long |
| Bholari | Large damage to aircraft hangar visible in Maxar imagery | High for structure damage | Presence/destruction of particular airborne warning aircraft inside |
| Shahbaz (Jacobabad) | Hangar and structure damage | High for historic physical damage | Specific aircraft inventory loss |
| Sukkur | Damage to hangar and apparent equipment area | Medium/high for structural effect | Complete function of facility or sustained operational disruption |
| Mushaf (Sargodha) | Runway impact/craters and subsequent repair imagery | High for historical runway change | Exact duration of closure and overall air capability |
| Sheikh Zayed/Rahim Yar Khan | Runway crater visible in historical imagery | High for runway damage | Whether damage prevented all military or civilian operations |

The source describes one expert's assessment that effects were **meaningful but not disabling** to the broader Pakistan Air Force. Other analysts viewed the operation as consequential. The imagery establishes physical change; strategic conclusions require follow-on performance evidence.

**Independent-review limitation:** This research session read the underlying Washington Post article, but **did not purchase/license original Maxar or Planet image files, inspect raw pixel data, independently register imagery dates or compute change scores**. Therefore the correct project classification is **supported but not fully established, high confidence** rather than independent original satellite forensic verification.

## Assertions specifically NOT validated

- A damaged hangar proves a named aircraft or crew was present when the structure was hit.
- A runway crater necessarily means a base was disabled for an extended period.
- A damaged mobile building proves loss of strategic command or nuclear control.
- Pakistan's claim that most missiles were intercepted proves no effective Indian hits.
- India's strike announcement proves all claimed buildings were destroyed.
- A commercial photo of a crater independently proves which weapon system caused it.

These extrapolations exceed what the imagery demonstrates.

## Exclusion of present-day exploitable data

Only broad public historic airfield names and non-actionable descriptions are recorded. This document omits coordinates, weapon attack azimuths, live sensor placement, command redundancies, air-defence inventories, exploit maps and detailed repair procedures.

## Core assessment

The publication of observable damage at six Pakistani airfields is significant evidence that India's subsequent military-facility strikes achieved **some kinetic effects**. It does not establish decisive suppression of the PAF, destruction of alleged high-value aircraft, or a complete strategic victory. This finding is symmetric with the project's insistence on authenticating Pakistani claims of damage at Indian bases.
