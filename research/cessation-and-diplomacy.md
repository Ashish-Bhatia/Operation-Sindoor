## What the 15:35 call actually establishes

India's Foreign Secretary on 10 May and DGMO Lt Gen Rajiv Ghai on 11 May said that Pakistan's DGMO initiated the conversation and the parties agreed to cease hostilities at 17:00. Their accounts provide a consistent official Indian timeline. The direct call recording, transcripts, original preceding telephone logs and any separate diplomatic messaging are not public in the reviewed record.

**Important distinction:** Initiating the documented **decisive call** is not necessarily proof of being the first participant to seek any end to the fighting. Pakistan's military representatives later asserted that India sought cessation, in apparent contradiction. The full original communications would be needed to determine who first made a specific proposal, who accepted it, and whether it was initiated through diplomatic channels before the military conversation.

## Third-country diplomacy: the causal question

[The White House compilation of 10 May statements](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-icymi-truth-social-x-posts-from-president-donald-j-trump-vice) confirms that the US administration publicly claimed senior-level contacts and a mediation role. Public Indian accounts consistently describe a bilateral DGMO understanding and challenge assertions that US economic pressure caused the cessation. Pakistani commentary has credited the US and other states.

The following propositions are not mutually exclusive:

- The US, other third countries and both governments communicated during escalation.
- The legally operative military cessation was agreed directly by the two DGMOs.
- A third party may have facilitated communication or exerted political pressure.
- The exact relative weight of these interventions remains uncertain.

Public announcements establish the *existence of a stated diplomatic position*, not the unrecorded causal mechanism of decision-making. A claim that third-country contacts were decisive requires participant records, contemporaneous evidence and credible alternative tests.

[Chatham House, 13 May](https://www.chathamhouse.org/2025/05/india-pakistan-ceasefire-remains-shaky-relations-unlikely-return-status-quo) outlines the differing political narratives; its expert analysis is not itself a firsthand log of the negotiations.

## Ceasefire implementation and subsequent violations

[Reuters on 10 May](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/pakistan-says-three-air-bases-targeted-by-indian-missiles-2025-05-10/) reported further firing and explosions after the cessation announcement. [India's Foreign Secretary's same-evening statement](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/pakistan-ceasefire-violationnews-live-updates-read-indias-full-statement-on-ceasefire-violations-by-pakistan-8382562/amp/1) accused Pakistan of repeat violations. Pakistani accounts disputed responsibility.

**Finding:** A perfectly incident-free first night is contradicted by credible observational reporting. Assigning individual violations to one state requires further incident-specific provenance; allegations alone do not establish authorship.

## May 18 expiry rumour and correction

Some reports implied the ceasefire would expire unless extended, citing remarks about earlier DGMO follow-ups. India's Army clarified on May 18 that the understanding reaffirmed on May 12 had no expiry. [Dawn's May 19 report](https://www.dawn.com/news/1911867) also described it as open-ended.

**Finding:** By May 18–19, both countries' public reporting supported a cessation without an automatic date of expiry. This was **not** a peace settlement, a final Kashmir settlement, or evidence that military alert levels were normal.

## Outstanding contradictions

1. Which side initially sought cessation, and through which channel?
2. When exactly did US and other governments' contacts occur relative to the 15:35 call?
3. What operational/diplomatic information prompted acceptance of cessation?
4. What were the precise contents of the DGMO agreement beyond the publicly disclosed stop order?
5. Which side caused each reported first-evening incident?
6. What were the actual troop drawdown measures, rather than merely proposed measures?
7. Are later public descriptions of the operation as 'paused' consistent with the open-ended ceasefire?

## Source and evidence audit

- Original public statements and verified reproductions form the documentary core.
- News outlet live-blog timestamps are often **publication times**, not event timestamps. They must not be used as exact event times without confirmation.
- US claims of diplomatic mediation and bilateral DGMO agreement should not be presented as logically exclusive.
- Underlying government call records, full archive/video records and bilateral signed terms have not been obtained.
- No monitoring task has been configured.

**Status:** Key public time anchors established. The motives, initial requester, third-party causal influence and responsibility for first-night incidents remain contested.
