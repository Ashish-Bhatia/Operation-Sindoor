# Aircraft-loss investigation: documentary and visual evidence review

**Edition:** Checkpoint 02 · **Cutoff:** 19 October 2026  
**Status:** Substantive independent review of published visual investigation and publicly recorded third-country military testimony. **Not** original image-forensic authentication or a complete loss roster.

## Scope and standard

The following distinct propositions are assessed independently:

1. An aircraft crashed or was destroyed.
2. The aircraft was an Indian military aircraft.
3. Its make/model and unique identity can be established.
4. It was brought down by enemy action rather than other causes.
5. The adversary, weapon and causal chain are established.
6. The loss occurred in a specific claimed air engagement.

Corroborating an aircraft crash does not necessarily corroborate the claimant's description of a shootdown or the rest of an asserted tally.

## Principal evidence, evaluated

### A. Washington Post visual investigation, 9 May 2025 (S38)

[Original reporting](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2025/05/09/fighter-jets-india-pakistan-attack/). Reporters Imogen Piper, Jarrett Ley, Evan Hill and Maham Javaid examined more than twelve images and videos, with three specialists. Their investigation presented material consistent with **at least two Indian fighter losses**, assessed to be a **Rafale and a Mirage 2000**, and a possible additional crash at Akhnoor whose type could not be identified.

**Rafale.** One wreckage image included recognizable tail markings and lettering associated with an Indian Rafale, compared in the article with historic photographs. The reporters could not independently geolocate that wreckage image. They identified local news of a crash response in the area where the wreckage was reportedly collected. The original media files, image hashes and serial records have **not** been independently obtained here. Assessment: **strongly supported Rafale loss**, high confidence; specific aircraft/incident metadata pending.

**Mirage 2000.** Aircraft specialists identified an external tank consistent with Mirage 2000 equipment. A tank by itself can be jettisoned without destroying the aircraft. However, proximity to separate reports of an aircraft hitting a school and a video showing suspected jet-engine debris added support for an aircraft loss. The investigators described this as a crash, not a fully demonstrated shootdown. Assessment: **supported Mirage 2000 loss**, medium confidence.

**Possible third aircraft.** Washington Post cited imagery and reports of another apparent crash in Akhnoor but could not identify its type. Counting it as a confirmed third unique Indian fighter would exceed the established public proof. Assessment: **unverified type/count**, low confidence.

**Causation limitation.** The Post's specialists did not determine whether the aircraft were shot down, crashed due to mechanical causes, or suffered other failure. It did not prove pilot fates.

### B. Reuters interviews with two US officials, 8 May (S11)

[Original Reuters report](https://www.reuters.com/world/pakistans-chinese-made-jet-brought-down-two-indian-fighter-aircraft-us-officials-2025-05-08/) attributes assessments to two unnamed US officials that Pakistan downed at least two Indian fighters. One identified a Rafale. Reuters did not publish the underlying intelligence, tracking data or wreckage images establishing causal details. This is separate reported external intelligence, **not** proof of a specific J-10 weapon-to-aircraft attribution for every claimed loss.

**Methodological note:** syndication of Reuters is not independent corroboration. Two US officials in one report need not represent two distinct collection streams.

### C. India's Chief of Defence Staff interview, 31 May (S12)

[Reuters interview](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/india-says-changed-tactics-worked-well-conflict-with-pakistan-2025-05-31/) reports CDS General Anil Chauhan **acknowledged initial Indian air losses** but withheld the number and types. He described adaptation in tactics. His on-record acknowledgment materially resolves the prior binary dispute over whether any Indian aircraft were lost. It does not validate the full Pakistani tally, a cause of individual crashes, or a claim that subsequent tactics were successful.

### D. French National Assembly original-language testimony, 9 July 2025 (S40)

[Official committee hearing record](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/opendata/CRCANR5L17S2025PO59046N082.html) records General Jérôme Bellanger, Chief of Staff of the French Air and Space Force, saying in French, *"un Rafale indien a été abattu"* (an Indian Rafale was shot down). In the same testimony he referred to a very large overnight air engagement and reported the account given to him by his Indian counterpart about prior warning to Pakistan.

**Evidentiary weight:** direct original public record, independent of Indian/Pakistani spokespeople, and a relevant senior military official from the producing country. The testimony increases confidence in the Rafale-loss assessment. The general did **not** present telemetry, a shot-specific forensic report, a full crash inventory or an identification of which missile or adversary platform caused that loss. His statement is evidence of his informed official assessment and cannot be transformed into a serial-specific judicial-grade finding.

**Translation check:** original French text examined. No machine translation substituted for the original.

### E. Pakistan's five-to-six aircraft claim (S11, S45)

Early Pakistani briefings reportedly stated **five** Indian aircraft shot down. The [16 May official Foreign Ministry statement](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/transcript-of-the-weekly-media-briefing-by-the-spokesperson-on-friday-16th-may-2025) asserted **six**. No individually authenticated five/six aircraft roster is available in this research pass. Record both historically and do not choose either by repetition.

## What is NOT implied

- The Rafale's loss is **not** a general statistical comparison of Rafale versus J-10 equipment quality.
- A PAF ground casualty is **not** proof of a destroyed PAF jet.
- A lost fuel tank is not a stand-alone aircraft kill.
- Multiple photographs of the same object are not multiple losses.
- A combat loss does not independently prove a specific air-to-air missile model, radar technique, rules of engagement or airspace violation.
- Pakistan's initial claims and India's later acknowledgment do not independently establish every detail each claimed.

## Evidence still required

- Original and timestamped copies of crash media, with safe historic provenance, copyright status and archived URLs.
- Independent audit of unique serials/markings where lawfully public.
- The Indian Air Force's own type-by-type incident or fatality record.
- Public foreign records clarifying the US and French claims.
- Civilian harm associated with each crash site separately from strikes and shelling.
- Specific documented Pakistani aircraft losses, or a defensible public statement that none has been evidenced.

## Editorial audit

Reversal test: The evidentiary rules used here are the same for any Pakistani alleged losses. The project will publish an independently validated Pakistani loss even if India did not claim it, and will reject an unsupported Indian claim even if it is repeatedly amplified.

**Correction control:** This replaces the narrower aircraft assessment of checkpoint 01. It does not assert that every aircraft loss is judicially proved or that an official three- or five-aircraft count is established.
