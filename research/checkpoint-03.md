# Operation Sindoor: investigative checkpoint 03

**Evidence cutoff:** 19 October 2026  
**Publication status:** Third source-critical research pass, not a completed commission report.  
**Focus:** Pakistan's Operation Bunyan-ul-Marsoos, contested strike outcomes, diplomatic cessation sequence, cyber/disinformation, and international law.

## Executive findings

### Finding 1: Pakistani military action is established; its full public damage claim is not

Pakistan's military publicly stated that its 10 May counteroperation engaged **26 Indian targets**. The announcement was reported in [Radio Pakistan's 12 May summary](https://www.radio.gov.pk/12-05-2025/armed-forces-honour-pledge-to-nation-against-indian-aggression-dg-ispr). Its [10 May account](https://www.radio.gov.pk/10-05-2025/pakistan-launches-operation-bunyan-un-marsoos-to-respond-indian-aggression) also presented broad destruction claims. These confirm the public positions and operating name, not that every target was actually hit or damaged.

The [Stimson Center's original 28 May evidence synthesis](https://www.stimson.org/2025/four-days-in-may-the-india-pakistan-crisis-of-2025/) found limited independently observable damage at Indian military facilities after the initial air engagements. However, [India's own 10 May morning briefing, preserved in the complete transcript](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/05/mil-250510-india-meab01.htm), acknowledged **limited equipment and personnel damage** at four air stations. It is therefore untenable to assert that either all Pakistani destruction claims were proven or Pakistan caused zero Indian military damage.

**Provisional classification:** Pakistan's counteroperation is verified as an event; the 26-target engagement claim is verified as a claim; large-scale claimed destruction is unsupported, while the extent of smaller effects remains incompletely established.

### Finding 2: An extraordinary nationwide cyber claim remains unsupported

Pakistan's [10 May state broadcaster account](https://www.radio.gov.pk/10-05-2025/pakistan-launches-operation-bunyan-un-marsoos-to-respond-indian-aggression) reported that cyber operations rendered **70% of India's electricity grid** dysfunctional. [India's same-day PIB fact-check, reproduced by Akashvani in Hindi](https://newsonair.gov.in/hi/%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0-%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%87-%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%B2-%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%A1%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE-%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%87-%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%BE/) rejected the claim.

No credible national outage accounting, independent grid-operator data or technical report validating the purported collapse has been acquired. This extraordinary **outcome** claim is unsupported. That does not rule out smaller cyber incidents or attempted intrusions, which need separate evidence and attribution.

### Finding 3: The timing of cessation is now specifically documented

- **10 May 15:35 IST (15:05 PKT, 10:05 UTC):** Indian DGMO Lt Gen Rajiv Ghai said he spoke to his Pakistani counterpart after earlier Pakistani hotline contact.
- **10 May 17:00 IST (16:30 PKT, 11:30 UTC):** Cessation effective time publicly announced by India and acknowledged by Pakistan.
- **10 May evening:** further incidents reported, with competing responsibility assertions.
- **12 May evening:** DGMOs spoke again about maintaining no firing or hostile actions and considering forward troop-reduction measures.
- **18 May:** Indian Army clarified the arrangement had no expiry date.
- **19 May:** Pakistani press reported officials of both countries viewing it as open-ended.

Sources: [Indian DGMO remarks via DD India](https://ddindia.co.in/2025/05/pakistan-proposed-ceasefire-violated-it-within-hours-indian-army/), [10 May government news account](https://newsonair.gov.in/india-pakistan-agree-to-ceasefire-after-dgmo-level-talks/), [12 May follow-up](https://newsonair.gov.in/india-pakistan-hold-dgmo-level-talks-agree-on-no-firing-troop-reduction-measures/), [Army no-expiry correction](https://newsonair.gov.in/indian-army-says-ceasefire-with-pakistan-is-open-ended-does-not-have-expiry-date/), [Pakistan independent reporting](https://www.dawn.com/news/1911867).

**Unresolved:** Exact contents of any preceding diplomatic approaches, each participant's motives, first proposer across every communication channel, and responsibility for after-17:00 incidents.

### Finding 4: An Indian official publication expressly refers to Pakistan approaching Washington

An official [PIB explainer published on **12 May 2025, 17:22 IST**](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?ModuleId=3&NoteId=154451&lang=2&reg=48) says Pakistan approached the United States to broker calm. The same government account cites the DGMO-to-DGMO cessation. This is a meaningful documentary nuance: a **Pakistani approach to the US** and **direct bilateral military agreement** are compatible. The fact of the statement does not verify the actual contact or US decisiveness.

The inquiry continues to distinguish diplomatic pressure, facilitation, mediation, an announcement, and direct military communication. No contemporaneous full negotiator call logs were acquired.

### Finding 5: Both states' sweeping percentage/destruction claims warrant downgrading

The same Indian PIB explainer asserts that **11 Pakistani air bases were destroyed** and **nearly 20%** of Pakistani air-force infrastructure was eliminated. The independent [Washington Post historical satellite investigation](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2025/05/14/india-pakistan-strikes-conflict-damage/) found physical damage at **at least six airfields**. It did **not** establish the total destruction of eleven entire air bases or validate a nationwide percentage. The percentage lacks a published denominator and consistent definition of destroyed. This is **unsupported** from the independently examined evidence.

Pakistan's claims that many Indian military installations were seriously destroyed face the same evidentiary problem. **Equivalence of standards does not mean equivalence of measured effects.**

### Finding 6: Preliminary legal analysis is possible, definitive verdicts are not

The [UN Charter Article 2(4)](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-1) prohibits interstate force, subject to relevant exceptions. [Article 51](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-7) concerns self-defence following an armed attack. India and Pakistan advanced competing defensive accounts. Relevant assessment requires evidence of armed attack, attribution, necessity, proportionality and the unsettled application of force against non-state groups across borders.

Separately, the [ICRC customary rules](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/customary-ihl/v1) require distinction, target verification, proportionality in attack and precautions. An allegedly militant compound cannot be designated wholly targetable because of a historical association without current object-specific evidence. Damage imagery does not disclose planners' ex ante knowledge.

Neither government's statements nor the currently reviewed public exhibits permit a final legal finding for every site.

### Finding 7: Avoid interpreting political terminology as formal chronology

Pakistan's [ISPR 12 May framing](https://www.app.com.pk/national/victorious-pakistan-names-19-day-military-conflict-with-india-marka-e-haq/) uses **Marka-e-Haq** for the crisis from 22 April to 10 May. **Operation Bunyan-ul-Marsoos** denotes its specific 10 May counteroperation. Thus the claimed nineteen-day conflict is a national narrative time span, **not proof of nineteen days of interstate kinetic combat**.

## Primary-source integrity and corrections

- **12 May 2025, 17:22 IST:** Direct re-examination of the PIB 'Global Solidarity with India' press note establishes the publication date of S79. It was provisionally misdated in an earlier data addition and was corrected before publication of this checkpoint.
- A May 10 Indian consulate source contains a conspicuously mislabeled/mixed heading with embedded April 23 material and a translation section; it was not treated as an uncontaminated stand-alone English military transcript. The military admissions were checked against the fuller documentary reproduction and specialist study.
- Repeated claims from a government and its state broadcaster are **not** independent source streams.
- Satellite imagery and archived original media were **not** downloaded or independently hashed; reports using that material are marked as secondary visual investigations.
- No ongoing autonomous monitoring or future execution has been scheduled.

## Remaining highest-value gaps

1. Full original public NIA case exhibits and later signed court orders.
2. Person-level civilian and military casualty reconciliation, with no double counting.
3. Independently authenticated airframe loss evidence and cause, for both sides.
4. Historical commercial imagery acquired under lawful license and technically inspected.
5. Original DGMO, Indian, Pakistani and US diplomatic communications underlying cessation.
6. A comprehensive 2026 source sweep and original Indus Waters awards review.
7. Data-based economic accounting, defence procurement attribution and long-term deterrence outcomes.
8. Independent nationwide grid operating records for May 10 and any documented technical cyber incidents.

## Conclusion at this checkpoint

The public record supports two military observations without requiring an absolute victory narrative: **Pakistan appears to have imposed notable initial Indian aircraft losses**, and **India demonstrably damaged Pakistani military infrastructure in the later confrontation**. Pakistan's extensive later destruction claims are not independently substantiated; neither is India's strongest retrospective infrastructure-destruction percentage. Direct DGMO cessation and international diplomatic contacts are not mutually exclusive.

The investigation is **unfinished**. Missing technical evidence is recorded as missing, not silently replaced by a zero or an authoritative assertion.
