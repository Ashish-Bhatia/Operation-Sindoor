**Evidence cutoff:** 19 October 2026  
**Status:** Open independent public-record investigation, not a definitive commission report.  
**Continuity:** This checkpoint extends [checkpoint 03](checkpoint-03.md), the specialist dossiers and [structured evidence register](../data/research.json). ## Executive assessment

Two propositions are supported by the reviewed evidence without endorsing either state's full victory narrative: India suffered initial aircraft losses, and Indian attacks inflicted visible physical damage at Pakistani military air installations. Pakistan's five/six aircraft tally and India's particular claimed Pakistani aircraft kills remain unverified at airframe level. Infrastructure damage is not interchangeable with destroyed aircraft, national force attrition or continuous operational shutdown.

The new evidence adds **(1)** France's written defence-ministry response, **(2)** a 2026 Indian Rafale fleet-support RFP dispute, **(3)** commercial-imagery reporting on reconstruction into 2026, **(4)** a source-level UN/NIA numerical discrepancy in the Pahalgam investigation, and **(5)** an August 2026 award and Indian jurisdictional rejection under the Indus Waters Treaty. Dates and evidentiary source types matter: an original official text establishes its own wording, whereas a media description of imagery is a *secondary visual analysis* unless the original data and metadata are independently examined.

## Finding A: Rafale loss and the procurement-document inference

The [French National Assembly's 25 November 2025 defence-ministry response](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/questions/QANR5L17QE6745), in answer to written question 6745, refers to the **presumed loss of an Indian Rafale** while saying the circumstances were not known and India had not confirmed details. It says an alleged failure of the Rafale's SPECTRA self-protection system was not documented. The questioner's assertion about a specific PL-15E / J-10C causal chain is a *question*, not a finding adopted by the ministry. This corroborates the dossier's existing [French air-force chief testimony and visual analysis](aircraft-loss-review.md), without converting the record into a serial-numbered official Indian loss declaration.

On [23 June 2026 India Today](https://www.indiatoday.in/india/story/air-force-iaf-document-36-rafales-pakistan-operation-sindoor-loss-claims-2932499-2026-06-23) reported an IAF request for proposals covering bridge support for 36 Rafale fighters. The paper treated that procurement figure as contradicting Pakistani Rafale-loss claims. **Caution:** a contract quantity or baseline fleet scope does not by itself show 36 uniquely identified airframes remained intact, serviceable or flying on the tender date. Conversely, neither the tender nor this criticism authenticates all claimed Pakistani shootdowns. Seek the original RFP, annexures, inventory serials and dated airworthiness entries. Do not infer the operation's tactical/technical missile kill mechanism from the French question text.

**Adjudication:** At least one Rafale loss is strongly supported by combined visual reporting and foreign official assessment; exact identity and cause remain incompletely proved. Pakistan's full tally is not independently established. The RFP does not negate the loss evidence.

## Finding B: Pakistani airbase effects, repair duration and denominators

The [Washington Post's 14 May 2025 satellite assessment](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2025/05/14/india-pakistan-strikes-conflict-damage/) described physical damage at at least six airfields, an observation already analysed in [the airfield dossier](airfield-damage-review.md).

[India Today's 6 May 2026 satellite review](https://www.indiatoday.in/india/story/operation-sindoor-pakistan-airbase-repairs-still-unfinished-after-a-year-satellite-images-show-2907688-2026-05-06) used ESA Sentinel-2 and Vantor imagery and assessed damage/reconstruction at multiple bases. Its report identifies Bholari, Sukkur, Murid, Nur Khan, Shahbaz/Jacobabad, Mushaf/Sargodha and Rahim Yar Khan. It expressly lacked imagery sufficient to establish damage at Rafiqui. These are important independent *published visual analyses*, not original pixel-level authentication by this repository.

[India Today's 18 September 2026 Mushaf revisit](https://www.indiatoday.in/india/interactive/story/490-days-satellite-images-show-brahmos-crater-at-sargodha-reopened-for-repair-2997799-2026-09-18) reported renewed works at a previously patched runway location. This conflicts only superficially with earlier reporting that a crater had been patched in 2025: temporary patching, later resurfacing and use of other runway portions are different events. The later visible work does **not** prove 490 days of continuous runway unavailability. Nor does a destroyed hangar automatically establish an aircraft was inside.

**Adjudication:** Significant localized Indian-inflicted physical damage is supported. Exact aircraft destroyed on the ground, cumulative repair costs, number of fully unavailable bases and the claimed destruction of 20% of PAF infrastructure remain unresolved.

## Finding C: Pahalgam attribution and attacker-count discrepancy

Paragraph 84 of [UN Security Council monitoring report S/2025/482, dated 24 July 2025](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2127961/n2517600.pdf), states that **five terrorists** attacked Pahalgam on 22 April and 26 civilians died. It says TRF claimed the attack on 22 April, repeated the claim on 23 April, then retracted it on 26 April. It presents *different member-state views* on the TRF/LeT relationship rather than a unanimous UN evidentiary determination of Pakistan-state involvement.

The Indian [NIA's December 2025 chargesheet announcement](https://nia.gov.in/nia-chargesheets-pak-based-lettrf-6-other-accused-pahalgam-terror-attack-case) and [6 July 2026 supplementary announcement](https://nia.gov.in/pak-based-lettrf-chief-hafiz-saeed-named-nia-supplementary-chargesheet-pahalgam-terror-attack) attribute the attack to named LeT/TRF defendants and three alleged direct gunmen. The apparent **UN five / NIA three** difference is a worthwhile forensic reconciliation target, but could reflect different levels of participant or changing investigative information. UN member-state-sourced reporting and subsequent Indian prosecution are not necessarily speaking about the same identified set of people. Secure original witness, ballistic, identification, digital-evidence and legal exhibits before judging which account is complete. A chargesheet is not a conviction.

**Adjudication:** The named LeT/TRF attribution is a serious documented prosecutorial theory, not a final judicial finding. No public record reviewed here independently proves that Pakistan's civilian executive or armed-forces command expressly ordered the attack.

## Finding D: Human cost and cessation process

The attack's **26 civilian victims** are distinct from deaths incurred during the subsequent interstate hostilities. The [Indian Defence Ministry's June 2026 statement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2278473&lang=1&reg=6) describes **six Indian military fatalities**. [Pakistani official figures as of 13 May 2025](https://www.app.com.pk/national/40-civilians-11-troops-martyred-in-indian-aggression-ispr/) list **40 civilians and 11 service personnel** dead. These are attributed, dated counts, *not* independently verified final bilateral fatalities and not comparable without date, category and duplicate controls. Indian civilian deaths from Pakistani shelling require a separate indexed reconciliation, as addressed in [the casualty dossier](casualty-reconciliation.md).

India's 10 May announcement places its DGMO contact at **15:35 IST** and the cessation at **17:00 IST**. A contemporaneous [US State Department statement attributed to Marco Rubio](https://www.publicnow.com/view/A565DB95F366DC3C09975C8B62B4140F689ADB2C) describes US diplomatic intervention. Both can be true. The direct telephone channel does not alone rule out foreign facilitation, and Washington's public language does not independently establish that US pressure uniquely caused the agreement. See [checkpoint 03's documented extended chronology](cessation-and-diplomacy.md).

## Finding E: 31 August 2026 Indus Waters Treaty award

On **31 August 2026**, the Court of Arbitration for the Indus Waters Western Rivers Arbitration (PCA Case 2023-01, PCA acting as registry) issued an award concerning India's decision to hold the treaty in abeyance and a separate interim-measures order concerning the Ratle project. The [PCA's public announcement](https://pca.int/en/news/pca-press-release-pca-case-no-2023-01-the-indus-waters-western-rivers-arbitration-islamic-republic-of-pakistan-v-republic-of-india-6/) and [award register](https://www.italaw.com/cases/37953) identify the documents. The tribunal held the treaty remained in force and rejected the asserted legal effect of unilateral abeyance. India's [official 31 August response](https://eoibelgrade.gov.in/index.php/listview/NzE2) rejected the tribunal's competence, denied the award's effect and affirmed its abeyance decision.

The accurate status is an **award plus contested jurisdiction and implementation**. Do not characterise India's position as endorsement of the award, or imply that the award by itself verifies how water was subsequently released. For deeper questions, inspect the full award, Treaty articles, competing legal briefs, and dam operational records.

## Priority acquisition queue for checkpoint 05

1. Dated tail-number aircraft inventories, crash-site images and chain-of-custody records from both air forces.
2. Full original June 2026 IAF RFP, supported quantities, contract terms and identified aircraft/airworthiness roster.
3. Licensed georeferenced Sentinel/Vantor scenes for each installation with image dates, spatial resolution, construction phase and valid control areas.
4. Primary Pakistani aircraft-loss records, casualty identities, repair and sortie logs, independently cross-linked to India's claimed kills.
5. Filed NIA court exhibits; reconcile UN para 84's five with NIA's three principal gunmen, and authenticate TRF claim/retraction.
6. Victim-level hospital/civil registration data on both sides, with dates, classifications and deduplication.
7. Original DGMO and diplomatic logs and signed decisions regarding May 10–18 cessation.
8. Full award, treaty text, India/Pakistan submissions, Ratle orders and subsequent implementation evidence.
9. Event-specific UN Charter and international-humanitarian-law assessments, separating legality of force from legality of individual attacks.
10. Quantitative economic, aviation, cyber, procurement and long-run deterrence assessments with audited evidence.

**Boundary:** No complete combat-score verdict, precise two-sided aircraft-destruction total, full civilian harm audit, public demonstration of Pakistan-state command authorization for Pahalgam, or blanket legal verdict is supported by this checkpoint. No ongoing autonomous monitoring is running.
