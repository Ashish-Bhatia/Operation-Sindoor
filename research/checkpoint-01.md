# Research checkpoint 01: Operation Sindoor

**Evidence cutoff:** 19 October 2026  
**Status:** Documentary first pass, not a completed investigation  
**Scope completed:** Core 22 April–10 May chronology; official statement comparisons; Pahalgam prosecution milestones; first aircraft-loss evaluation; preliminary satellite-damage leads; cessation contradiction; 2026 Indus Waters arbitration and an attributable aviation-cost forecast.  
**Scope not completed:** Forensic reconstruction, original chargesheet exhibits, individual loss roster, casualty deduplication, full airbase image analysis, comprehensive legal and economic assessment, and full late-2026 document sweep.

## Executive assessment

1. The attack near Pahalgam on 22 April 2025 killed at least 26 people. The UN Security Council condemned it on 25 April without naming an organization responsible in its press statement. Source: [S01](../data/research.json).
2. India's first Operation Sindoor release was posted by PIB at **01:44 IST on 7 May** and reported **nine targeted sites**, explicitly saying **no Pakistani military facilities** were targeted in the opening action. This is a documented contemporaneous public position, not proof of the nature or results of each target.
3. India claimed a targeted response to terrorist infrastructure, based on intelligence attribution and an asserted need to pre-empt further attacks. Pakistan denied the Indian rationale and alleged attacks on civilian areas. Both sets of claims require independent incident-level analysis.
4. Reuters reported on 8 May that **two US officials** said Pakistan had downed at least two Indian jets, including a reported Rafale. On 31 May, India's Chief of Defence Staff acknowledged **initial air losses** in a Reuters interview but offered **no tally or types**. Thus some Indian losses are established, while exact claims about five or six aircraft are not established here. [Reuters 8 May](https://www.reuters.com/world/pakistans-chinese-made-jet-brought-down-two-indian-fighter-aircraft-us-officials-2025-05-08/) · [Reuters 31 May](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/india-says-changed-tactics-worked-well-conflict-with-pakistan-2025-05-31/).
5. Pakistani authorities publicly announced Operation Bunyan-ul-Marsoos. India and Pakistan agreed to a cessation on 10 May. Washington publicly claimed mediation and extensive engagement, while India emphasized DGMO-to-DGMO communication and denied that external pressure determined its decision. Neither proposition alone rules out the other.
6. Commercial satellite imagery **reported by news outlets** showed physical damage at multiple Pakistani military facilities. Original licensed images have not yet been downloaded, registered or pixel-inspected in this investigation, so precision destruction claims are premature.
7. NIA's 15 December 2025 announcement of a chargesheet against LeT/TRF and six other accused and its 6 July 2026 supplementary filing naming Hafiz Saeed extend the public prosecution record. The filings are accusations, not court findings of guilt.

## Case 01: Pahalgam

### Established

- **Incident date:** 22 April 2025.
- **Deaths:** At least 26, as independently acknowledged by the United Nations on 25 April. The NIA later categorized the deceased as 25 tourists and one local civilian.
- **Official Indian attribution before strikes:** The 7 May Foreign Secretary briefing alleged LeT/TRF involvement and referenced investigative communications and eyewitness work.
- **Subsequent prosecution:** NIA announced chargesheets on 15 December 2025 and 6 July 2026.

### Not established in this document

- Detailed minute-by-minute attack timings, names and identities of all attackers, underlying digital forensics, full ballistic chain of custody, the totality of precursor warnings, any specific decision-maker's negligent conduct, and verdicts on accused individuals.
- The government publicly described Operation Mahadev on 28 July 2025 and forensic linkage to the April attack. The full underlying forensic record has **not** been inspected independently.

## Case 02: Initial strikes

India's earliest public description and the subsequent state counterclaims must be treated as separate evidence. The initial nine publicly identified targets were Sawai Nala, Syedna Bilal, Gulpur, Barnala, Abbas, Sarjal, Mehmoona Joya, Markaz Taiba at Muridke, and Markaz Subhanallah at Bahawalpur. The names and alleged organizational connections come principally from India's briefing, summarized contemporaneously by Reuters.

What is established: that these sites were named as Indian targets and that the two governments publicly disagreed over their nature.

What is not yet established comprehensively: each site's actual use on the strike date, lawful status under international humanitarian law, individually verified destruction, complete civilian casualties, and reported militant deaths.

## Case 04: Broader military strikes and physical effects

India's opening description of no Pakistani military sites targeted concerns the **7 May initial action**, not necessarily the later confrontation. Indian and Pakistani official accounts reflect later fighting with reported missiles, drones and airbase strikes.

News outlets reported Maxar and other commercial satellite imagery showing physical damage at Nur Khan, Murid, and several other bases. Structural change is a narrower conclusion than claims of destroyed aircraft, killed aircrew, full radar failure or causal leverage over negotiations.

- [Maxar imagery reporting, 13 May](https://www.indiatoday.in/amp/india/story/satellite-images-pakistan-air-bases-nur-khan-india-operation-sindoor-2723934-2025-05-13).
- [Maxar imagery reporting, 28 May](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/new-satellite-images-show-damage-caused-to-pakistan-nur-khan-and-murid-air-bases-under-operation-sindoor-101748420304677.html).

**Pending:** obtain imagery lawfully, verify dates and image licensing, record each site condition before/after and consider explanations other than a reported strike.

## Case 05: The cessation and the United States

- The [US administration's public statement](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-icymi-truth-social-x-posts-from-president-donald-j-trump-vice) of 10 May asserts US mediation and high-level contact.
- [India's later PIB account](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2128748) states Pakistan's DGMO called India's DGMO and that cessation took effect at 17:00 IST.
- [Pakistan's account](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/recent-developments-and-ceasefire-between-india-and-pakistan) records the agreement and describes its own operation as self-defence.
- [Reuters, 28 July](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/defence-minister-denies-india-bowed-pressure-end-fighting-pakistan-2025-07-28/) documents Indian rejection of outside pressure as the decisive cause.

**Finding:** Existence of the understanding is verified. The diplomatic process and causal role of foreign intervention remain contested. Direct military communication and US diplomatic outreach could coexist.

## Case 06: Indus Waters Treaty arbitration (through 31 August 2026)

The treaty dispute did not conclude when military firing ceased.

- **23–24 April 2025:** India announced that it would hold the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance after the Pahalgam attack. Pakistan's National Security Committee rejected the legal validity of unilateral suspension and imposed reciprocal aviation, trade and diplomatic measures. [Pakistan NSC original statement](https://nsd.gov.pk/NewsDetail/ZDdhYjc3ZjktODZmYS00NGVlLTg3YmYtMTVkMjc4ZjJiYzBh).
- **27 June 2025:** The PCA case docket lists a supplemental award on competence, relevant to the jurisdiction dispute. Its complete PDF has not been reviewed in this edition.
- **31 August 2026:** The PCA published a formal notice that a Court of Arbitration had issued an [award on the status of the Indus Waters Treaty and order on interim measures concerning Ratle](https://pca-cpa.org/en/news/pca-press-release-pca-case-no-2023-01-the-indus-waters-western-rivers-arbitration-islamic-republic-of-pakistan-v-republic-of-india-6/). The [PCA case docket](https://pca-cpa.org/en/cases/284/) lists relevant decisions. The official PCA website blocks direct access for this investigation, and full underlying awards have **not** been independently examined.
- **31 August 2026:** India's [Ministry of External Affairs statement, via Embassy of India](https://eoibelgrade.gov.in/index.php/listview/NzE2), rejects the constitution and jurisdiction of the tribunal and says India's treaty-abeyance position remains in force. This original statement was directly reviewed.

**Finding:** The tribunal's award, India's rejection and the continuation of the dispute are established public events. No definitive independent holding on the legal effect is adopted without reading the full award and considering India's jurisdiction objection. The PCA administers arbitral proceedings; it should not be conflated automatically with the particular tribunal or described as a merits-deciding party.

## Case 07: Aviation and economic spillovers

Reuters [reported on 1 May 2025](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/air-india-sees-pakistan-airspace-ban-costing-it-600-million-over-12-months-seeks-2025-05-01/) that Air India's letter to the Civil Aviation Ministry estimated about **US$600 million of additional costs if Pakistan's airspace closure lasted 12 months**. The primary company letter was read by Reuters but has not been independently retrieved here.

This is a **conditional, forward-looking forecast**, not an audited actual expenditure. Longer routes, fuel and crew costs are identified mechanisms. The number is not an all-India cost estimate and should not be included as realized expenditure without later company and aviation records. [Reuters, 15 October 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/air-india-forms-team-drive-cost-savings-memo-shows-2026-10-15/) describes multiple pressures on the airline, making causal attribution of aggregate financial losses to the 2025 crisis invalid without further analysis.

## Case 08: Stated objectives, revised official accounts and phase boundaries

The first [7 May 01:44 IST PIB announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2127370) limited India's opening targets to nine described terror sites and explicitly excluded Pakistani military facilities in the opening phase. The government broadcaster's [contemporaneous briefing summary](https://newsonair.gov.in/press-briefing-on-operation-sindoor/) reported **01:05–01:30 IST** as the opening strike window; precise individual impact times remain unverified.

The [8 May 14:34 IST Ministry of Defence briefing](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2127670) asserted Pakistani drone and missile attacks overnight, Indian counterstrikes on Pakistani air-defence radars, a neutralized Lahore system and **16 civilians killed by Pakistani shelling** by that date. These are verified statements by India, not independently audited military effects or deduplicated casualty entries.

On **28 July 2025**, Defence Minister Rajnath Singh [described in Lok Sabha](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2149356&lang=2&reg=48) the overall politico-military goal as punishing what India described as Pakistan's proxy war, avoiding capture of Pakistani territory, and destroying terrorist infrastructure. He said India estimated **more than 100 alleged militants killed** and that the campaign had been **paused rather than ended**. His further [29 July Rajya Sabha remarks](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2149793&lang=1&reg=3) reiterated that the operation was halted but not ended.

**Evidence rule:** The 28–29 July accounts are valuable for the government's public rationale but are *retrospective*. They cannot silently replace the narrower aims stated before or during the opening 7 May phase. Claimed militant deaths and the alleged destruction of air-defence systems remain subject to incident-level verification. The 16 civilian fatalities reported by India on 8 May are a dated provisional claim, not the final civilian toll.
